# Exploratory and Live, Programming and Coding: A Literature Study Comparing Perspectives on Liveness

## Reference

Patrick Rein, Stefan Ramson, Jens Lincke, Robert Hirschfeld, and Tobias Pape.
“[Exploratory and Live, Programming and Coding: A Literature Study Comparing
Perspectives on
Liveness](https://doi.org/10.22152/programming-journal.org/2019/3/1).” *The
Art, Science, and Engineering of Programming* 3(1), Article 1, 2019; first
published online 23 July 2018.

## Contribution

The study distinguishes the overlapping traditions of exploratory
programming, live programming, and live coding and compares their motivations,
domains, desired run-time outcomes, research contributions, and evaluation
methods.

## Method

The authors retrieve literature from three major indexing services, select a
sample of 212 publications, and perform thematic analysis plus bibliographic
comparison. The corpus gives broad evidence about research emphases rather
than evaluating one environment or deriving a security architecture.

## Findings

- “Liveness” names several goals, including immediate feedback, changing a
  running system, exploratory tool use, and performance; these should not be
  treated as one feature.
- Exploratory-programming work emphasizes general-purpose tool and technical
  designs, whereas live coding spans broader motivations and empirical work in
  performance art.
- Live-programming research commonly aims to improve accessibility and
  comprehension and often evaluates designs with users.
- A credible live environment therefore needs an explicit claimed benefit and
  matching evaluation rather than measuring only update latency.

## Relevance

Atom OS should profile liveness separately for observation, pure evaluation,
view editing, actor code replacement, schema migration, and system-service
update. Each mode needs distinct authority, safe points, outcome evidence, and
human evaluation.

## Limits

The taxonomy reflects its search terms, databases, coding scheme, and 2018
cutoff. It does not show that live tools are usable by non-programmers or that
a particular update mechanism is safe, resilient, or secure.
